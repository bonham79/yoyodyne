# English past tense verbs (`eng`)

Author(s): Kristysha Chan, Kyle Gorman

Source(s): Apertium (via UDLexicons, via CityLex), CELEX (via CityLex), UniMorph (via CityLex)

## Summary

This data consists of English verbs and their "simple" (preterite) past tense form. Most English verbs are so-called "regular" verbs forming a past tense in _-ed_, though there are more than a hundred "irregular" verbs which either have zero (i.e., identical to the stem) past tense forms, like _hit_/_hit_, or undergo various stem changes (i.e., ablaut, as in _sing_/_sang_, or shortening with dental suffixation, as in _sleep_/_slept_). This example is inspired by recent papers by Kirov & Cotterell and Corkery et al. (and much earlier, Rumelhart & McClelland and the reply from Pinker & Prince).

## Third column

The third column simply records whether the verb is "regular" or "irregular" as commonly understood; this should also allow interested parties to study the irregular past tense and ignore the regular.
